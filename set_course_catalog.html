Recommendations

1
Confirm credit values from the official syllabus. All credits in this catalog are estimated at 3 cr. for technical courses, 2 cr. for language/civic courses, and 1 cr. for Sports, based on the standard LMD convention. BUIB may allocate differently — obtain the official course catalog from the registrar before seeding production data.
2
Confirm 8 unidentified course names. BCB201, BCB203, BCB205, PWB201, UPB201, UPB203, CTB321, and CTB323 appear in the timetable grids but their full names are not listed in any of the 5 provided PDFs. Request individual department syllabuses for BCB, PWB, UPB, and the full CTB L300 description sheet.
3
Verify CIB303 vs CIB313. Both are named "Construction Materials" — CIB303 (L300, Sem 1) and CIB313 (L300, Sem 2, from the 2nd-semester exam timetable). These may be sequential modules (Part I / Part II) with different content, or CIB313 may be a misprint for CIB303. Clarify before seeding.
4
ETB program appears incomplete. Only 6 ETB courses are identified (L200 Sem 1 + one L300 Sem 1 shared course). ETB is likely a full 3-year program; its L200 Sem 2, L300 Sem 2, and all L400 courses are absent from the provided PDFs. Request the ETB-specific timetable sheets.
5
EEB L300 Sem 2 appears incomplete. Only one course (EEB310: Sequence Control) is identified for EEB in L300 Sem 2. An engineering L300 semester typically has 5–8 courses. The remaining EEB L300 Sem 2 courses are not in the provided PDFs.
6
BCB, PWB, and UPB data is sparse. These three programs collectively have 19 courses vs. 95 for CEB+CTB alone. Their complete timetables — especially L300 Sem 1, all L400, and for UPB and PWB the bulk of their program — were not in the provided documents. A separate timetable document likely exists for these built-environment programs.
7
Database seeding strategy for shared courses. Create one course record per unique course code — so CEB205 and CTB203 become two separate records (they have different codes, different departments), both with isShared: true. During timetable generation, the scheduler must treat these as the same physical class: same room, same time slot, same lecturer. Storing the partner code(s) in a sharedCodes or notes field lets the engine identify and enforce this constraint.
8
L400 course volume is heavily skewed toward CEB/CTB. CEB has 27 L400 courses, CTB has 19, while EEB has 6 and MEB has 6. This could reflect deep specialisation tracks (CEB splits into Software and Networks tracks at L400 — courses 401–415 are software-track, 421–437 are networks-track), or it could mean L400 Sem 2 data for EEB and MEB is missing. Verify with respective HODs.
9
CEB L400 has two visible specialisation tracks. Codes CEB401–CEB418 are Software Engineering focused; CEB421–CEB437 are Networks/Telecommunications focused. These may be elective tracks rather than courses all students take simultaneously — confirm whether students choose one track or attend both streams, as this affects room scheduling significantly.
10
Seed order matters for foreign keys. When writing seed.ts, insert in this order: School → Department → Lecturer → Course → (timetable entries last). All course records need a valid departmentId before they can be inserted. Universal courses (FRE, ENG, SPT, CVE) should be assigned to a "General Education" department record, or assigned to each department separately if the schema requires one department per course.
